Urban Agriculture: The Future of Cities

As the global urban population continues to swell, city planners and environmentalists are turning their attention to innovative methods of food production. Urban farming, which encompasses everything from small community gardens to high-tech vertical farms, is fast becoming a crucial element in sustainable city living.

Vertical agriculture utilizes controlled environmental conditions where crops are grown in stacked layers without natural soil. By carefully managing light, humidity, and nutrient distribution through advanced hydroponic systems, vertical farms can achieve up to ten times the yield of conventional rural farming per square meter.

However, high startup expenses and continuous electricity consumption remain considerable hurdles. Critics argue that until renewable energy powers these installations completely, their environmental footprint might undermine the benefits of reduced food transportation miles.

Questions 1–3: True / False / Not Given

Question 1

Vertical farms require traditional soil to maintain crop health.

Answer: FALSE
ব্যাখ্যা: প্যাসেজের ২য় প্যারায় বলা হয়েছে: "...crops are grown in stacked layers without natural soil." অর্থাৎ মাটি ছাড়া চাষ হয়।
Question 2

Vertical farming can produce significantly higher yields per unit area than regular farming.

Answer: TRUE
ব্যাখ্যা: প্যাসেজে উল্লেখ আছে: "...can achieve up to ten times the yield of conventional rural farming per square meter."
